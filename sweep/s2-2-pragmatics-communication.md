# LLM->human boundary: pragmatics, discourse, and communication theory

Field slice: Gricean maxims/implicature, audience/recipient design, grounding, speaker-meaning without a speaker, sincerity/speech acts, authorship and accountability (Hancock AI-MC agenda), relevance theory, register/politeness, discourse coherence, and message-mediated studies. 22 entries, 2020+ preferred with named foundational theory included.

---

- **H. P. Grice. "Logic and Conversation." In *Speech Acts* (Cole & Morgan, eds., *Syntax and Semantics* vol. 3), 1975. https://doi.org/10.1163/9789004368811_003.** Founding statement of the Cooperative Principle and the four maxims (Quantity, Quality, Relation, Manner), and of conversational implicature as inference over an assumed cooperative, intentional speaker. The LLM->human relevance: every later paper on hedging, verbosity, or "why does this read as off" implicitly asks whether Grice's maxims, defined for a speaker with beliefs and intentions, even apply when there is no single intending source behind the tokens. open-access: no (rights-restricted reprint); no direct PDF found.

- **Herbert H. Clark, Susan E. Brennan. "Grounding in Communication." In *Perspectives on Socially Shared Cognition*, 1991. https://doi.org/10.1037/10096-006.** Defines common ground and grounding as incremental, costed joint action between participants who coordinate both content and process (turn-taking, acknowledgments, repair) to establish mutual belief that an utterance has been understood. This is the theoretical yardstick against which "does a chatbot ground" is measured: an LLM turn is generated without the collaborative process of contribution the theory assumes, so backchannels/repairs it produces are simulated, not evidence of an actual updated joint model. open-access: no; no direct PDF found.

- **Deirdre Wilson. "Relevance Theory." In *The Oxford Handbook of Pragmatics*, 2016 (author's self-archived version dated 2015 in OpenAlex). https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780199697960.013.25.** Restates Sperber & Wilson's relevance-theoretic account: comprehension is an inferential process that picks the interpretation yielding adequate cognitive effects for minimal processing effort, guided by the presumption that an utterance is optimally relevant *to the hearer, from the speaker*. Applied to LLM output, the presumption of optimal relevance is a claim about a speaker's intention to be relevant to this hearer — a claim an LLM cannot underwrite the same way, since it has no stake in the hearer's cognitive economy, only a statistical proxy for it. open-access: no; no direct PDF found.

- **Allan Bell. "Language Style as Audience Design." *Language in Society*, 1984. https://doi.org/10.1017/s004740450001037x.** Establishes audience design: speakers style-shift primarily for the addressee, secondarily for auditors/overhearers, and style variation is response to audience rather than an independent dimension. LLM output is generated for a diffuse, often unknown "audience" inferred from a prompt or persona instruction rather than a live interlocutor being tracked turn by turn, so any audience design it exhibits is a prior (RLHF-shaped) approximation, not Bell's live-accommodation mechanism. open-access: no; no direct PDF found.

- **Maurice Jakesch, Jeffrey T. Hancock, Mor Naaman. "Human Heuristics for AI-Generated Language Are Flawed." *PNAS*, 2023. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2208839120.** Five experiments (total N in the thousands) show people's folk heuristics for detecting AI-written self-presentations (deli descriptions, dating bios, etc.) are near chance and systematically miscalibrated — people mistake formality/fluency cues for humanness. Directly evidences that readers lack a reliable cue for the sender-side authorship question central to sincerity conditions and accountability. open-access: yes, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2208839120 (PNAS OA).

- **Maurice Jakesch, Advait Bhat, Daniel Buschek, Lior Zalmanson, Mor Naaman. "Co-Writing with Opinionated Language Models Affects Users' Views." *CHI*, 2023. https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3581196.** Online experiment (N=1,506) shows an opinionated LLM writing assistant shifts what participants write and subsequently believe, while participants underestimate the model's influence on them. Shows the sender's "intent" behind AI-mediated text can be partly the model's statistical lean rather than the human author's, undermining speaker-meaning attribution at the point of composition. open-access: yes, https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3544548.3581196.

- **Robert Dale, Ehud Reiter. "Computational Interpretations of the Gricean Maxims in the Generation of Referring Expressions." *Cognitive Science*, 1995. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1902_3.** Foundational NLG paper formalizing several computational readings of the Gricean maxims for referring-expression generation, arguing the cheapest ("Incremental") algorithm best matches human output despite not perfectly satisfying Quantity. Establishes that "Gricean-sounding" generation has always been an engineered approximation, not maxim-following in Grice's intentional sense — a caveat that carries straight into LLM pragmatics claims. open-access: yes, https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1207/s15516709cog1902_3.

- **Paula Rubio-Fernández. "Overinformative Speakers Are Cooperative: Revisiting the Gricean Maxim of Quantity." *Cognitive Science*, 2019. https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12797.** Four production experiments show human speakers are routinely over-informative (redundant color/size adjectives) when it aids listener efficiency, reframing over-informativeness as cooperative rather than a maxim violation. Relevant baseline for LLM verbosity critiques: over-informativeness alone doesn't mark AI text as different from human speech; the diagnostic question is whether the extra information tracks a modeled listener's need the way human redundancy does. open-access: yes, https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1111/cogs.12797.

- **Mateus de Souza Monteiro, Vinícius Carvalho Pereira, Luciana Cardoso de Castro Salgado. "Investigating Politeness Strategies in Chatbots through the Lens of Conversation Analysis." *IHC (Brazilian Symposium on Human Factors in Computing Systems)*, 2023. https://doi.org/10.1145/3638067.3638068.** Conversation-analytic case study of chatbot transcripts finds politeness strategies applied inconsistently relative to the face-threat level of the exchange, producing register mismatches users notice. Empirical grounding for the "register/politeness mismatch" symptom readers report with AI-mediated text. open-access: yes, https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3638067.3638068.

- **Yi-Chia Wang, Alexandros Papangelis, Runze Wang, Zhaleh Feizollahi, et al. "Can You be More Social? Injecting Politeness and Positivity into Task-Oriented Conversational Agents." arXiv, 2020. https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14653.** Trains conversational agents on politeness/positivity strategies mined from human customer-service dialogue and shows injecting them changes user engagement metrics. Shows politeness in machine-mediated text is a tunable knob decoupled from sincerity, i.e. can be dialed up independent of any underlying communicative intent — a mechanism behind the "performed" feel of AI politeness. open-access: yes, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.14653.

- **Philip R. Cohen, C. Raymond Perrault. "Elements of a Plan-Based Theory of Speech Acts." *Cognitive Science*, 1979. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0303_1.** Foundational computational-pragmatics paper modeling speech acts as planning operators intended to change a hearer's beliefs, treating illocutionary force as derivable from a planning agent's goals rather than requiring a metaphysically "sincere" speaker. This is the historical root of treating speech-act felicity as an engineering property, which is exactly what licenses (and what critics contest about) attributing "assertions," "promises," etc. to an LLM with no persisting committed self. open-access: yes, https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1207/s15516709cog0303_1.

- **Fiona Draxler, Anna Werner, Florian Lehmann, Matthias Hoppe, Albrecht Schmidt, Daniel Buschek. "The AI Ghostwriter Effect: When Users Do Not Perceive Ownership of AI-Generated Text But Self-Declare as Authors." *ACM TOCHI*, 2023. https://doi.org/10.1145/3637875.** Two studies (n1=30, n2=96) find users of AI writing assistance do not feel ownership/authorship of the resulting text internally, yet still publicly present it as their own without disclosure. Direct evidence for the authorship/accountability gap: the sincerity condition (the presenter stands behind the words) survives publicly even though the private sense of authorship has collapsed. open-access: yes, https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3637875.

- **Jiaqi Zhu, András Molnár. "Blissful (A)Ignorance: People Form Overly Positive Impressions of Others Based on Their Written Messages, Despite Wide-Scale Adoption of Generative AI." arXiv, 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15678.** Pre-registered online experiment (N=647) applying signaling theory: readers still credit AI-assisted messages to the sender's personal qualities, forming overly positive impressions of writers who used GenAI, because verification cost for authenticity is high. Shows the reader-side failure mode complementing Jakesch/Hancock 2023: not just "can't detect AI text" but "still over-attributes character/effort to the named human sender" — the accountability fiction holds even after credibility should be discounted. open-access: yes, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.15678.

- **Mark Dingemanse, Seán G. Roberts, Julija Baranova, Joe Blythe, Paul Drew, Simeon Floyd, et al. "Universal Principles in the Repair of Communication Problems." *PLoS ONE*, 2015. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0136100.** Cross-linguistic conversation-analytic survey (12 languages, 8 families) finds "other-initiated repair" (the recipient flags trouble, the speaker fixes it) is a universal, tightly-timed structure of human dialogue. Sets the human-human repair baseline against which AI-mediated exchanges are compared: a chatbot's "repair" is typically a re-generation triggered by explicit re-prompting, not the same turn-taking-embedded, low-latency joint repair machinery documented here — a candidate mechanism for perceived "hollowness" in extended AI dialogue. open-access: yes, https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0136100&type=printable.

- **Vidya Setlur, Melanie Tory. "How Do You Converse with an Analytical Chatbot? Revisiting Gricean Maxims for Designing Analytical Conversational Behavior." *CHI*, 2022. https://doi.org/10.1145/3491102.3501972.** Wizard-of-Oz study of a data-analysis chatbot, using the four Gricean maxims as design/evaluation lenses for its responses to ambiguous or underspecified queries. Treats the maxims explicitly as engineering targets for LLM/chatbot dialogue design rather than as descriptive facts about a communicating mind, illustrating the field's move from "does the model obey Grice" to "how do we make it perform obeying Grice." open-access: yes, https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3491102.3501972.

- **Sunnie S. Y. Kim, Q. Vera Liao, Mihaela Vorvoreanu, Stephanie Ballard, Jennifer Wortman Vaughan. "'I'm Not Sure, But...': Examining the Impact of Large Language Models' Uncertainty Expression on User Reliance and Trust." *ACM FAccT*, 2024. https://doi.org/10.1145/3630106.3658941.** Empirical study of how different hedging/uncertainty phrasings in LLM outputs affect user reliance and calibration of trust, finding hedges shift behavior but not always in the intended (better-calibrated) direction. Directly addresses hedging as a manipulable surface feature of LLM text disconnected from an underlying epistemic state, versus human hedges which (per Gricean Quality) are usually read as signaling the speaker's actual confidence. open-access: yes, https://doi.org/10.1145/3630106.3658941.

- **James W. A. Strachan, Dalila Albergo, Giulia Borghini, Oriana Pansardi, Eugenio Scaliti, Saurabh Gupta, et al. "Testing Theory of Mind in Large Language Models and Humans." *Nature Human Behaviour*, 2024. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-01882-z.** Battery of ToM tasks (false belief, irony, faux pas, hinting, etc.) comparing GPT-4/LLaMA2 output to human performance; LLMs matched or exceeded humans on some tasks but showed distinct error patterns (e.g., irony, faux pas). Bears on whether an LLM "models the reader" the way audience/recipient design requires: apparent ToM competence on test items doesn't establish that the model tracks a specific reader's evolving state during generation, only that it can simulate ToM-flavored inference on demand. open-access: yes, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-01882-z.

- **Kyle Mahowald, Anna A. Ivanova, Idan Blank, Nancy Kanwisher, Joshua B. Tenenbaum, Evelina Fedorenko. "Dissociating Language and Thought in Large Language Models." *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 2024. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2024.01.011.** Cognitive-science position paper arguing formal linguistic competence (fluent, grammatical output) is separable from functional competence (reasoning, world-modeling, communicative intent), and that LLMs are strong on the former without guaranteeing the latter. This is the clearest theoretical handle for "speaker-meaning without a speaker": fluent sentence-meaning production can run without any of the functional/intentional machinery that speaker-meaning theories (Grice, relevance theory) presuppose. open-access: yes, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11416727/pdf/nihms-2021156.pdf.

- **Robert E. Wolfe, Aayushi Dangol, JaeWon Kim, Alexis Hiniker. "Toward Needs-Conscious Design: Co-Designing a Human-Centered Framework for AI-Mediated Communication." *AAAI/ACM AIES*, 2025. https://doi.org/10.1609/aies.v8i3.36751.** Interview study with N=14 certified Nonviolent Communication trainers plus a diary co-design study with N=13 lay users, building a framework (Intentionality, Presence, Reciprocity) for AI-mediated communication design grounded in NVC theory. A direct 2025 continuation of the Hancock AI-MC research agenda, translating grounding/sincerity concerns into concrete design pillars for tools that write on a person's behalf. open-access: yes, https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIES/article/download/36751/38889.

- **Josephine Hagedorn, Roman Klinger, Kai Sassenberg. "AI-Based Writing Assistants for Emotional Tone: Investigating Users' Acceptance and Recipients' Perceptions in Online Negotiations." *Cyberpsychology: Journal of Psychosocial Research on Cyberspace*, 2026. https://doi.org/10.5817/cp2026-4-1.** Experimental study of AI tone-adjustment in negotiation messages, measuring both senders' acceptance of AI suggestions and recipients' perceptions of the resulting (recipient-blind-to-AI-origin) messages. Directly tests the paper's core question empirically: recipients judge AI-mediated tone differently once AI involvement is salient, separating the text's apparent register from the sender's actual affective state. open-access: yes, https://cyberpsychology.eu/article/download/41122/35877.

- **Andreas Komninos. "AI-Mediated Messaging: Attitudes, Expectations and Violations in Close Relationships." SSRN, 2026. https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7244859.** Survey-based study of attitudes and expectation violations when partners in close relationships discover messages were AI-assisted or AI-generated, framed around relational communication norms. Extends AI-MC accountability findings (Draxler et al., Zhu & Molnár) to the highest-stakes relational context, where the felt violation is specifically about who is accountable for intimate-register speech acts. open-access: yes (SSRN preprint), https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7244859 (landing page; direct PDF gated behind SSRN download click).

- **Han Cheol Moon, Tasnim Mohiuddin, Shafiq Joty, Chi Xu. "A Unified Neural Coherence Model." *EMNLP-IJCNLP*, 2019. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/d19-1231.** Proposes a neural model unifying local (entity-grid-style) and global coherence signals to score/detect topic drift and sentence-ordering problems in generated or human text. Supplies the technical apparatus later NLG-evaluation papers use to operationalize "discourse coherence" and detect the topic drift readers report as a marker of generated text over longer spans. open-access: yes, https://www.aclweb.org/anthology/D19-1231.pdf.

## Synthesis: why LLM->human differs (pragmatics/discourse mechanisms)

1. **No committed speaker behind speaker-meaning.** Grice's cooperative principle and Cohen & Perrault's plan-based speech acts both presuppose an intending agent; Mahowald et al.'s formal/functional dissociation gives the mechanism for how fluent sentence-meaning can be produced without that agent — explaining why LLM text can be grammatical and coherent yet feel "unauthored."
2. **Sincerity/accountability detaches from the visible sender.** Draxler et al. (AI Ghostwriter Effect) and Komninos show the human sender keeps public accountability for AI-assisted text even as private authorship-feeling collapses; Zhu & Molnár shows readers still over-credit the named sender — together these describe a persistent "accountability fiction" that AI-mediation strains but doesn't break.
3. **Readers have no reliable detection heuristic.** Jakesch, Hancock & Naaman (PNAS 2023) show near-chance human ability to spot AI-generated self-presentations, meaning the pragmatic adjustments readers would want to make (discount sincerity, recalibrate implicature) usually can't be triggered consciously.
4. **Recipient/audience design is simulated, not live.** Bell's audience design and Strachan et al.'s ToM testing frame the gap: an LLM can produce ToM-consistent output on demand but has no continuously updated model of *this* addressee the way a human interlocutor accumulates one turn by turn.
5. **Grounding and repair are structurally different.** Clark & Brennan's grounding and Dingemanse et al.'s universal other-initiated-repair describe tightly-timed, jointly-costed processes; AI-mediated exchanges substitute re-prompting for that joint repair work, a candidate source of the "hollow" quality readers report.
6. **Over-informativeness/verbosity is not inherently anomalous.** Rubio-Fernández shows human speakers are routinely over-informative too; the LLM-specific tell is when extra content doesn't track a modeled listener's need (unlike human redundancy), which studies like Setlur & Tory try to operationalize via engineered Gricean maxims.
7. **Hedging is a tunable surface feature, decoupled from epistemic state.** Kim et al. show hedge phrasing in LLM output changes user reliance somewhat independent of actual model confidence, unlike human hedges which are normally read as calibrated signals under Quality.
8. **Politeness/register can be injected without sincerity.** Wang et al. and de Souza Monteiro et al. show politeness strategies are trainable/tunable knobs in dialogue systems, producing register that is well-formed but decoupled from face-work motivation, which readers detect as mismatch.
9. **Discourse coherence over long spans is a separately engineered property.** Moon et al.'s coherence modeling underlies the empirical detection of topic drift — a marker distinct from sentence-level fluency and specific to extended generated discourse.
10. **The research agenda is actively catching up (2025-2026).** Wolfe et al. and Hagedorn et al. show the field moving from diagnosing the gap to designing for it (Needs-Conscious Design, tone-assistant acceptance/perception studies), continuing Hancock's original AI-MC agenda beyond the already-covered 2019/2020 Hancock/Jakesch base papers.
